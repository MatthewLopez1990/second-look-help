Second Look Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 17, 2026

Last updated: November 17, 2026

This policy covers the Second Look app for iPhone. In the App Store, the app is called "Second Look: Family Scam Check."

The short version

  • Second Look checks messages on your iPhone.
  • We, the maker of Second Look, do not run a server. We do not get your messages, pictures, or answers.
  • There are no ads. There is no tracking. You do not make an account with us.
  • Something leaves your phone only when you choose to send it. For example, you can ask a family member in your family circle, or text someone you trust.
  • The family circle uses Apple's iCloud. What you send there is kept in iCloud, not by us. We cannot read it.

Who we are

Second Look is made by Matthew Lopez, an individual app developer in the United States.

Email: mattappleid793@gmail.com

In this policy, "we" and "us" mean Matthew Lopez.

What Second Look does

Second Look helps you check a text message, an email, or a phone call that worries you. It answers with one of three results: "No warning signs found," "Not sure," or "Likely a scam." It gives a reason and one thing to do. You can then ask a family member, or someone you trust, for a second look.

What stays on your phone

These things are kept on your iPhone, inside Second Look. We cannot see them.

  • The messages you check. This includes the words, the links, who it says it is from, and the picture if you checked a screenshot.
  • Second Look's answers. This is the result, the reasons, and the advice.
  • Your answers to questions. Sometimes Second Look asks you questions, for example about a phone call. Your answers stay on the phone unless you choose to send a check to someone.
  • Your settings. For example, whether answers are read out loud, Simple mode, and how far you got in setup.
  • Someone you trust, if you add one. This is one person's name and phone number, and their small contact picture if they have one. You pick them from your contacts or type them in.
  • Random ID numbers. The app makes them up on your phone. They are not your phone's serial number or any ID from Apple. They let the app tell your checks and your circle apart.

How Second Look checks a message

Second Look uses its own rules, which run on your phone. On iPhones that have Apple Intelligence, it also uses Apple's language model that runs on the phone itself. Your message is not sent to Apple or to us to be checked.

Pictures

  • If you pick a picture yourself, Second Look uses the iPhone's photo picker. Second Look gets only the picture you pick.
  • If you take a screenshot of a message and come back, Second Look can find that newest screenshot for you. To do this, it asks once if it may look at your photos. If you say yes, it looks only for recent screenshots.
  • The words in a picture are read on your phone by the iPhone's own text recognition. Your pictures are not changed and are not uploaded.
  • You can say no. You can then pick the picture yourself. You can change your choice any time in the iPhone Settings app.

Things you copy

Second Look can notice that you copied something, so it can offer to check it. To do this, it only asks the iPhone whether something was copied. It reads what you copied only when you tap the Paste button.

Contacts

Second Look does not ask to see all your contacts. When you add someone you trust, the iPhone shows its own contact list. Second Look gets only the one person you tap.

Reading out loud

"Read this to me" uses the voices on your iPhone. The words are not sent anywhere to be spoken.

Phone calls

Second Look never listens to your calls. It never records them. To check a call, it asks you questions, and uses only your answers.

Widgets and the Control Center button

The Second Look widgets and the Control Center button only open the app. They show no messages and no results.

Sharing into Second Look

You can share a link, an email, a PDF, or a picture from another app into Second Look. It is checked on your phone. It is kept in a private space on your phone that only Second Look can use. Second Look may show a notice on your phone, such as "Tap here to see your check in Second Look." That notice is made on your phone and is not sent anywhere.

Backups

Like other apps, Second Look's saved checks and settings are part of your iPhone backup, if you use one. Your backup is kept by Apple in iCloud, or on your own computer. We cannot see it.

What can leave your phone, and only when you choose

Nothing in this list happens unless you tap a button to do it.

Asking your family circle

The family circle lets a family member give you a second opinion inside the app. It uses Apple's iCloud. Everyone in the circle needs an iPhone with Second Look and must be signed in to iCloud.

Making a circle and inviting people. One person makes the circle, usually the family member who helps. Second Look then makes an invitation link. That person sends the link with the iPhone's share menu, for example by text. Each link lets one person join. A circle holds up to five people. When you open an invitation, iCloud tells Second Look the name of the person who invited you, as it appears in their Apple Account, so the app can show who invited you.

What a member shares with the circle. Each member shares a plain label ("Family member"), whether they help or get help, and whether their phone can run Apple's on-device model. Setup does not ask for your name or a photo.

What is sent when you ask. When you tap "Ask" on a result, Second Look shows who will see it. When you then tap Send, these go to your circle:

  • the message you checked, its links, and who it says it is from;
  • the picture, if you checked a screenshot;
  • for a phone call, your answers about the call;
  • what Second Look found: the result, the reasons, and the advice.

What comes back. Your helper answers "It's fine," "It's a scam," or "Call me first," and may add a short note. If your phone could do only a basic check, and your helper's phone can run Apple's on-device model, your helper's phone may check the message again on that phone and share its fuller answer with the circle. The circle also keeps small records of when a request was sent, seen, answered, or taken back.

Where it is kept. It is all kept in the private iCloud space of the person who made the circle, in Apple's CloudKit service. It is shared only with the people who joined that circle. Second Look saves nothing in any public part of iCloud. Each member's phone also keeps a copy of the circle's requests and answers.

Encryption. The words of the message, its links, the result and reasons, the answers, and the notes are saved in iCloud's encrypted fields. Pictures are saved as iCloud files. Some small details are not in encrypted fields: the random ID numbers, dates and times, and whether a request is open, seen, or taken back.

Who can see it. Only the people in your circle. We cannot read your private or shared iCloud data. Apple stores it under Apple's own privacy policy and iCloud terms.

Notifications. To tell your helper about a request, and to tell you about an answer, iCloud sends a notification through Apple's notification service. The words are always the same, and never include the message or anyone's name:

  • "Someone in your circle wants a second look."
  • "Your helper answered your second look."

You choose whether to allow notifications. Second Look asks during setup, and you can change it any time in the iPhone Settings app. iCloud also sends silent signals that tell the app to fetch new requests and answers. They show nothing on screen.

Taking it back. You can tap "Take it back" until your helper opens the request. The message is then removed from the circle.

Texting someone you trust

When you tap "Ask" for someone you trust, Second Look opens a text in the iPhone's own Messages app. The text holds the message you checked and Second Look's answer. It may include the picture. Nothing is sent until you tap Send. The text goes through your phone company or Apple's iMessage, like any text you send. We never see it.

When you tap "Call," your phone calls that person. We never see the call.

Asking an AI app

On the "Everything I checked" page, you can tap "Ask an AI app too." First, Second Look shows a notice. It says that the company that makes the AI app will see the message.

If you continue, the iPhone's share menu opens. You choose the app. Second Look hands it a question that holds the message you checked (and the picture, if there is one) and what Second Look found. You can also tap "Copy the question" and paste it yourself.

After that, the AI company handles the message under its own privacy policy. Second Look does not read that app's answer. We never see the message or the answer.

Reporting a scam or getting help

Second Look can show places to report a scam, like the Federal Trade Commission. If you tap a link, it opens in your web browser. If you tap a phone number, your phone calls it. If you tap an email address, your email app opens a new email for you to write and send. Second Look does not send them anything for you. What you tell them is up to you.

Opening other apps

Second Look can open Messages, Mail, or another email app so you can find a message. It only opens the app. It does not read anything inside that app.

Writing to us

If you email us, we get what you write. In Settings, "Family circle status" has a Copy button for a short report you can paste into an email to us if the circle is not working. That report holds no messages and no names. It is sent only if you send it.

What we never get

  • We never get your messages, emails, pictures, contacts, or answers.
  • We do not run a server that could read them.
  • We cannot read what your family circle keeps in iCloud.
  • We do not sell or rent any information.
  • We do not show ads.
  • We do not track you across apps or websites.
  • We do not use outside analytics, crash reporting, or advertising tools.
  • You do not make an account with us.

What Apple may share with us

Apple runs the App Store and handles the purchase of the app. We never get your card or bank details. If you agreed in your iPhone settings to share data with app developers, Apple may give us crash reports and summary numbers, like how many people opened the app. Apple decides what these hold. They do not include your messages. You can turn this off in the iPhone Settings app: tap Privacy & Security, then Analytics & Improvements.

Children

Second Look is not made for children under 13. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children. The app does not send us any personal information.

How long information is kept

  • On your phone: until you delete it, or delete the app.
  • In your family circle's iCloud space: until the message is taken back or deleted, or until the circle ends. The small records of when a request was sent or answered stay until the circle ends.
  • With us: we do not get your information, so we keep none. If you email us, we keep your email only as long as we need it to answer you.
  • Texts and AI apps you send to: they keep what you send under their own rules.

How to delete your information

  • One check: in Second Look, tap More, then Past checks. Under the check, tap Delete. If you sent it to your circle, Second Look offers to delete it "here and on" your helper's phone. Choose that. It also removes it, and the answers to it, from the circle.
  • Leave a circle: open Settings. Under Family circle, tap "Leave this circle." The circle's requests and answers are removed from your phone. Messages you sent earlier stay in the circle until they are deleted or the circle ends. To remove them, delete them first, as shown above.
  • End a circle: if you made the circle, open Settings and tap "End this circle." This ends it for everyone and removes its requests and answers from iCloud. Checks saved on each phone stay on that phone.
  • Someone in your circle: if you made the circle, you can remove a person from the "Your family circle" screen. They stop getting requests, and the circle's requests leave their phone.
  • Someone you trust: open Settings, tap the person, then tap Remove.
  • Photo access: in the iPhone Settings app, tap Apps, then Second Look, then Photos.
  • Notifications: in the iPhone Settings app, tap Notifications, then Second Look.
  • Everything on your phone: delete the Second Look app. This removes everything it kept on your phone. It may still be in older iPhone backups, which you control. Deleting the app does not end your family circle. If you made a circle, end it in Second Look first.

We do not have an account for you, so there is no account to delete.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, laws may give you rights to see, fix, or delete information about you. Second Look keeps your information on your phone and in your own iCloud, so you can see and delete it yourself, as shown above. We do not hold any of it. If you have a question, please write to us.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will post the new version on this page and change the date at the top. If a change means more information leaves your phone, the app will tell you before it happens.
